## The four findings that should change the plan

**1. Naturehike is the franchise, and nobody in Australia owns it.**
`naturehike` returns 2,900 monthly Australian searches. That is larger than every beginner
and value keyword in this study combined. The brand plus its models accounts for roughly
6,000 of the 18,060 monthly searches measured. The top organic result for `naturehike cloud
up review` is a five-year-old American video. Naturehike itself and a local importer buy ads
against the query. There is no Australian voice.

**2. Australian Google demand for "budget hiking gear" barely exists. Australian YouTube
demand for it is enormous.**
`cheap backpacking gear` is 10/mo. `budget backpacking gear` is 10/mo. `chinese hiking gear`,
`cheap hiking setup` and `hiking gear under 500` all return 0. Meanwhile the same phrases
carry multi-million-view videos on YouTube and a 283-subscriber Australian channel got 42,000
views on a price-comparison Short. **The Bush Gums thesis is a browse proposition, not a
search proposition.** Build videos on it, not pages.

**3. Price contrast in the title is the highest-leverage packaging decision available.**
Bec Cliffe: 283 subscribers, `$64 vs $570 Rain Pants`, 42K views, 148x. Jack Brookes: 14.9K
subscribers, `Backpacking Gear I'd Never Buy Again` at 207K and `Stop Overspending on Solo
Backpacking Gear` at 163K, against 25K for an excellent Overland Track guide on the same
channel. The format works without an audience and outperforms destination content by roughly
8x on identical channels.

**4. Never publish a single-product review where a head-to-head is possible.**
Dose of Dirt, one channel, one category, one production style: four budget sleeping bags
tested together at 178K, one budget sleeping pad reviewed alone at 16K. An 11x spread purely
on format. Bush Gums' own data model is already a two-kit comparison.

**Only one new page is recommended: a Naturehike Australia hub.** It is the one cluster where
demand (2,900/mo brand plus roughly 3,000/mo across models) is not addressable by any
existing page, and where a canonical topic page is clearly justified rather than a
thin keyword variant. Everything else in this plan maps to a page that already exists.

**Do not create** pages for `cheap backpacking gear`, `hiking gear under 500`, `chinese
hiking gear`, `best value hiking gear`, `budget hiking gear` (a variant of `cheap hiking
gear` with identical planner metrics), `hiking stove australia` (no catalogue product), or
any city-modifier page. Four of those have zero measured demand and the rest have no page to
send anyone to.

**Schema considerations.** Product pages already carry structured research data and are
natural `Product` plus `Review` candidates, but only once field testing has actually
occurred. Marking up a review rating before Bush Gums has used the product would contradict
the site's own stated evidence policy and misrepresent the page to Google. Hold review
schema until the trip footage exists. `VideoObject` on embedded videos and `FAQPage` on the
existing FAQ are safe now. `ItemList` suits the kit pages.

## YouTube to site funnel

```
Short (price contrast, no CTA)        -> discovery, no audience required
  -> Long-form video (chaptered)      -> YouTube search and browse
    -> description link + on-screen   -> Bush Gums landing page
      -> product research page        -> evidence, sources, open questions
        -> external merchant          -> affiliate click
```

Google organic runs the same funnel in reverse: a person searching `hiking sleeping mat`
lands on the R-value guide, reads the evidence, and either clicks through or watches the
embedded video. The two channels share the same middle.

**The measurement question this raises, and cannot currently answer:** the site has no
analytics and no email capture by design. Bush Gums cannot presently tell which traffic
source converts. That is a deliberate architectural choice in the repository, not an
oversight, and changing it is an owner decision outside this document. The 90-day learning
objectives below are written so that most of them can be answered from YouTube Studio alone.

The pattern worth internalising: **volume and revenue are inversely correlated in this
catalogue.** The highest-volume terms point at the cheapest products. The most valuable
products sit behind brand and model terms with modest volume. The kit content, which has no
search volume at all, points at four products simultaneously and is therefore the best
cart-value play the site has.

## Recommended publishing cadence

### Option A: one long-form video per week

- 12 to 13 long-form videos in 90 days
- Requires roughly 6 overnight trips in 90 days to stay honest, or heavy reliance on
  non-trip content
- Editing load: 4 to 6 days of work per video against a 7-day cycle, for one person
- Weather risk: a bad fortnight breaks the schedule with no buffer
- **Verdict: not viable.** The production system document costs a single trip cycle at 14
  days. Option A assumes 7. It will either collapse or force content that claims field
  experience Bush Gums has not had, which is the one failure the site cannot afford.

### Option B: one long-form video every two weeks plus three Shorts per week

- 6 to 7 long-form videos in 90 days
- 36 to 39 Shorts in 90 days
- Requires 3 to 4 overnight trips, which is achievable
- Matches the 14-day trip cycle exactly, with 2 to 3 buffer days built in
- Puts effort where the evidence says returns are highest: a 283-subscriber channel got 42K
  on a Short, while a 27.8K-subscriber Australian channel got 32K on its best long-form video
- **Verdict: recommended.**

### The recommendation

**Option B.** Long-form every second Thursday. Shorts on Monday, Wednesday and Saturday.

The deciding argument is not workload, it is credibility. Bush Gums' entire differentiation
is that it does not claim experience it has not had. A weekly long-form schedule, run by one
person through Australian weather, creates constant pressure to publish a verdict before the
trip that justifies it. Option B removes that pressure structurally.

## 90-day plan

Assumes one person, starting from zero subscribers, with eight publishable videos available
before the first trip.

### Weeks 1 to 2: publish before you hike

- Publish Video 1 (`$446 kit`) and 6 Shorts from the at-home capture session
- Complete the full before-trip checklist for both kits: every item weighed on camera,
  every packed size against the same reference object
- Set up the channel: banner, about copy, affiliate disclosure consistent with `/affiliate-disclosure`
- **Trip 1 booked** for week 3, mild-weather, close to home, low logistics

### Weeks 3 to 4: first trip and the Naturehike claim

- **Trip 1.** Film the full on-trail and morning checklist.
- Publish Video 2 (`Naturehike in Australia`) plus 6 Shorts
- Update the four Naturehike product pages with real first-use evidence and open questions
- Build the proposed `/guides/naturehike-australia` hub page

### Weeks 5 to 6: the trip payoff

- Publish Video 6 (`Everything That Went Wrong`) plus 6 Shorts from trip 1
- Update every product page carried on trip 1
- **Trip 2 booked** for week 7, different conditions from trip 1

### Weeks 7 to 8: comparison content

- **Trip 2.**
- Publish Video 3 (`Cheap vs Expensive Tent`) plus 6 Shorts
- Embed videos 1, 3 and 6 on `/starter-kits` and the tent guide

### Weeks 9 to 10: the uncontested query

- Publish Video 5 (`Anaconda vs Kathmandu vs AliExpress`) plus 6 Shorts
- Refresh `retailer-comparison.ts` prices and dates before publishing. The video is only as
  credible as the freshness of that data.
- **Trip 3 booked** for week 11

### Weeks 11 to 12: product depth

- **Trip 3.**
- Publish Video 4 (`Cloud Up Pro 1P review`) or Video 7 (`$17 vs $86 mat`), whichever has
  the stronger footage, plus 6 Shorts

### Week 13: review and decide

- Publish Video 10 (`$218 vs $446`) plus 6 Shorts
- Run the learning objectives review below
- Decide quarter two based on what the data says, not on this document

**90-day totals:** 7 long-form videos, roughly 42 Shorts, 3 overnight trips, 1 new page,
10-plus product page updates.

### Learning objectives for the first 90 days

Each is answerable from YouTube Studio or from merchant-side affiliate reporting, without
adding analytics to the site.

1. **Which title proposition produces the highest impressions click-through rate:** an
   explicit dollar figure (`$446`), a regret framing (`What Went Wrong`), or a brand and
   model name (`Cloud Up Pro`)? Videos 1, 6 and 4 test exactly this.
2. **Does cheap-versus-expensive outperform model-specific review** on this channel, as it
   does on Jack Brookes' and Dose of Dirt's? Compare video 3 against video 4.
3. **Do Shorts drive long-form views, or do they run as a separate audience?** Check the
   subscriber source and traffic source breakdown after 40 Shorts.
4. **Does Australian localisation improve performance,** or is it neutral? Compare the
   AU-specific framings against the generic ones in the same period.
5. **Which product produces the most affiliate clicks per thousand views?** Expect the tent
   or the mat on the model above. If it is the pillow, the value model in this document is
   wrong and should be rebuilt.
6. **Which video type generates returning viewers?** Watch whether the trust content
   (video 6) outperforms the browse content on return rate even where it loses on raw views.
7. **Is the Naturehike bet real?** If video 2 and the hub page do not build search impressions
   on `naturehike` terms within 90 days, the largest assumption in this plan needs revisiting.

## What not to waste time on

- **Pages for zero-volume keywords.** `chinese hiking gear`, `cheap hiking setup`, `hiking
  gear under 500`, `best value hiking gear`, `how to choose hiking gear`. All returned 0 AU
  volume. Several are excellent video titles. None are page targets.
- **City and state pages.** `hiking gear sydney` (390/mo) and `hiking gear melbourne` (90/mo)
  are people looking for a shop to walk into.
- **Kmart and Aldi content.** `kmart camping gear` at 1,000/mo is the biggest cheap-gear
  signal in Australia and there is no affiliate path to either retailer.
- **Stove content.** There is no stove in `src/data/catalog.ts`.
- **Separate pages for close variants.** `cheap hiking gear`, `budget hiking gear`,
  `inexpensive hiking gear`, `cheap hiking equipment` and `cheap trekking gear` returned
  byte-identical planner metrics. They are one page.
- **Chasing `camping pillow` volume.** 1,900/mo of largely car-camping intent pointing at a
  19 AUD product.
- **Destination and trail-beauty content as a growth strategy.** Every channel measured shows
  it underperforming gear content by 5x to 8x, including the Australian ones.
- **Production upgrades.** Nothing in the research connects equipment quality to performance
  in this niche. A 148x Short was filmed on a table.
- **Weekly long-form publishing.** See the Option A analysis.

## The one risk worth naming

This entire plan leans on Naturehike. Nine of the top ten keyword opportunities are Naturehike
terms, the recommended hub page is a Naturehike page, and two of the first five videos are
Naturehike videos. That concentration is what the Australian demand data actually says, and
ignoring it to look balanced would be dishonest.

But it means Bush Gums is building audience and search authority around a brand it does not
control, cannot guarantee supply of, and has no commercial relationship with beyond an
affiliate link. The mitigation is not to dilute the bet. It is to make sure the Bush Gums
*method* is the thing people remember, so that the audience follows the judgment rather than
the brand. That is what videos 6, 11 and 18 exist to do, and it is why the trust content
should not be deferred just because the browse content performs faster.
